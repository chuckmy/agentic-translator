# Style guide — Literary translation

## Voice and stance
Voice is the primary unit of fidelity. If the source narrator has a particular distance, irony, or quietness, that must be preserved even at the cost of word-level literalness.

## Sentence rhythm
Match the cadence of the source where possible. If a source sentence is short and clipped, the target sentence should be too. Avoid expanding terse Japanese into florid English (or vice versa).

## Cultural specifics
- Preserve culture-specific terms when they are evocative (紅葉, 着物, 舞妓) rather than substituting bland equivalents. Do not add an in-line gloss unless comprehension would otherwise fail.
- Place names: keep in the source-language form (祇園 → Gion, not "the Geisha District"; the Common → コモン).

## Sensory details
Preserve all sensory specifics — colors, sounds, textures, light. These carry the narrative weight; do not omit or generalize them.

## What to avoid
- Over-explanation. The source's restraint is part of the meaning.
- Adding qualifiers that the source does not have.
- Summarizing instead of translating.
- Naturalizing dialogue tags too aggressively when the source uses bare quotation.

## Foreignization vs. domestication
Lean foreignization on cultural elements; lean domestication on syntactic patterns (do not preserve un-English clause order or un-Japanese sentence-final structures).
